### From the lab to the community
As Director of the NSUK Entrepreneurship Development Centre, she has turned entrepreneurship education into a practical engine of self-reliance.

#### COMMUNIVERSITY
Her flagship initiative takes the University's knowledge, resources and innovation directly to host communities. It launched in July 2025 in Angwan Lambu, Keffi, where the team combined hands-on paint-formulation training with the renovation of a community mosque.

#### Skills for Self-Reliance
Digital skills (web development, programming, prompt engineering, UI/UX, content creation, video editing and AI tools) sit alongside vocational skills such as baking, welding, soap making, perfume blending, shoe making, resin art, agro-processing and furniture making.

#### Agripreneurship
The EDC rice farm runs from cultivation through harvest to milling at the Keffi Rice Mill, with local women taking part. It demonstrates agribusiness value chains in practice. In her words, each grain represents the Centre's commitment to agricultural innovation.

01 Biography

## A life devoted to knowledge and impact
From Universiti Putra Malaysia to the International Islamic University Malaysia and home to Nasarawa State University, Keffi.
Prof. Rashidah Funke Olanrewaju
Citp, FCRIDE, SMIEEE, MIET, MNCS, MAITP, MIAENG, MWIE, Fellow Erasmus+, PhD, MSc.IE, DIS(IIUM), BSC. (Hons.) (UPM)
A historic first
First Professor, and first female Professor, of Computer Science at Nasarawa State University, Keffi
Profile
Professor Rashidah Funke Olanrewaju is a Nigerian scholar, entrepreneur and thought leader in Artificial Intelligence, Software Engineering, Humanitarian Engineering, and Entrepreneurship for Sustainable Development. She was born in Kaduna, Nigeria. She is the first Professor of Computer Science at Nasarawa State University, Keffi (NSUK), and the first woman to hold that title, a milestone that inspires women in science, technology and academia across Africa.
Her education began at Government Secondary School, Babanla, Kwara State, and continued with a National Diploma in Computer Studies from Kaduna Polytechnic in 1996. She then read Computer Science at Universiti Putra Malaysia, graduating with a B.Sc. (Hons.) majoring in Software Engineering in 2003, and took a Postgraduate Diploma in Islamic Studies at the International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM) in 2001. She earned an M.Sc. in Computer and Information Engineering from IIUM in 2006, and a Ph.D. in Engineering there in August 2011, specialising in information security in digital image processing. Her doctoral work identified watermark embedding strength and produced an intelligent digital watermarking system based on the Safe Region of the Fast Fourier Transform, hiding a watermark in the safe region of the host image without distorting it.
Her professional career began at IIUM in 2003 as a tutor and demonstrator in the Faculty of Engineering. She became a part-time lecturer in 2009 and a post-doctoral fellow in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering in 2011, was appointed Assistant Professor in 2012 and Associate Professor in 2018, and served as Visiting Associate Professor at Ahmadu Bello University, Zaria, from 2015 to 2017. She coordinated the Software Engineering Research Group at IIUM from 2016 to 2021 and was a member of the pioneering EDC Startup Team, championing capacity-building and digital transformation for academic staff. She returned to Nigeria as Professor of Computer Science at NSUK in 2022.
At NSUK she serves as Director of the Entrepreneurship Development Centre (EDC). She has brought artificial intelligence, innovation and digital literacy into its business incubation and skill-acquisition programmes. Under her leadership the EDC has become a hub for tech-driven entrepreneurship, agro-innovation and creative enterprise training, giving students, artisans and women entrepreneurs AI-powered tools for business intelligence, financial inclusion and sustainable growth. The Centre has trained over 10,000 learners, launched the COMMUNIVERSITY initiative, and been recommended by TETFund to other institutions as a model of excellence.
She is also Deputy Director of the Open and Distance e-Learning Centre (ODeL), where she champions the move to digital learning so that education is flexible and accessible to diverse learners. As Head of the Intelligent Software Engineering Research Group (ISERG), she leads research in AI, secure computing, fintech innovation, humanitarian technology and sustainable network systems. She was Team Lead for the NSUK Post-UTME Digital Screening Project, a university-wide system with AI support for verification, course selection, payments and communication that made admissions more transparent and efficient.
Her scholarship spans more than 170 publications journal articles, conference papers, technical reports, book chapters and a textbook cited over 1,200 times, in venues such as IEEE Access, MDPI Information, the Malaysian Journal of Computer Science and Springer's Lecture Notes series. As of 2026 she has 97 Scopus-indexed documents, the highest of any scholar listed at Nasarawa State University, Keffi, ahead of the next entry at 92. She holds four registered intellectual-property filings in digital watermarking and secure e-banking, and has led or co-led eighteen competitive research grants. Her paper “Sustainable Development through Islamic Finance: The NutriBite Initiative's Impact on Hunger and Malnutrition” won the Best Paper Award at ICiFIL 2024 in Malaysia. She is a Co-Investigator on the British Council-supported WIDEN project on women's digital empowerment in Nigeria.
She is also an entrepreneur. She is a co-founder of Deep Socket Limited, a software development company in Nigeria, and she founded and runs RadiantRay Exclusive, a fashion and lifestyle brand that combines creativity with empowerment. She founded Women Lifeline International, an NGO that pioneers community-based economic empowerment programmes such as Goat Banking and NutriBite, rooted in the Islamic finance principles of waqf and sadaqah.
An advocate for ethical technology, she mentors at Minaret Muslim Connect (MMC), guiding young Muslims in research ethics, innovation and faith-based leadership, and writes at AI Nature's Open Lab on AI ethics, gender inclusion and digital equity. She is a Senior Member of IEEE Women in Engineering and serves on the Editorial Board of the SCIREA Journal.
Beyond her professional titles, she is a mother, a practising farmer who keeps an integrated snail, poultry and vegetable garden, a teacher at heart, a coach on women's health and wellness and a relationship counsellor who supports individuals, couples and families. She speaks English, Yoruba, Hausa and Bahasa Malaysia fluently and reads Arabic, loves cooking she has created recipes of her own enjoys tennis and basketball, and volunteers widely.
Educator, reformer and mentor, Professor Olanrewaju brings together intellect, faith and enterprise. Her work at the intersection of AI, entrepreneurship and humanitarian innovation reflects her conviction that technology should serve humanity and knowledge should drive sustainable change.

An inaugural lecture is one of the most important moments in a professor's career. In it, a newly appointed professor presents their field and their contributions to it to the University community and the public, and sets out a vision for the future.

### Nura-X
Described as the world's first Muslim female robot
A humanoid robot designed and built in Keffi, Nigeria, by Prof. Rashidah Funke Olanrewaju and the Intelligent Software Engineering Research Group she leads at Nasarawa State University, Keffi.
Nura-X is presented as an example of Nigerian innovation that carries local cultural and religious identity rather than importing someone else's. It was conceived, designed and built in Keffi, by a Nigerian team, in a Nigerian university laboratory.
The robot came out of the Intelligent Software Engineering Research Group (ISERG), the research group Prof. Olanrewaju has coordinated at NSUK since 2021, whose work spans artificial intelligence, secure computing, fintech and humanitarian technology.

## Discipline, Punctuality and Full Participation
Commencement of ENTC 311 Practical Training, 2025/2026 Session
She warned that no student should pay money to facilitators except through the Centre's official channels.
The Entrepreneurship Development Centre began practical training for ENTC 311 in the 2025/2026 session, as 300-level students moved from classroom learning to hands-on skill development. Prof. Rashidah Funke Olanrewaju addressed the students at the opening session and explained how the practical phase would run.
She stressed discipline, punctuality and full participation, and said the Centre would keep a structured, professional learning environment that prepares students for real-world opportunities.
For accountability, the Director introduced a barcode-based attendance system. Every student now has a unique barcode, scanned electronically at each session, so that attendance records are accurate and transparent. She reminded students that attendance would be monitored closely, because it counts towards their evaluation and their eligibility for certification.
Students had also been placed automatically in project groups for the coming exhibition. The system generated the groups to keep them fair and balanced. Each group will work together throughout the practical phase to develop, carry out and present a project that shows the skills its members have learned.
Finally, the Director gave a firm warning: no student should give money to any facilitator under any circumstances. All payments for the programme must go only through the Centre's official channels, and any request for a personal payment should be reported to management at once. The measures, she said, reaffirm the EDC's commitment to transparency, accountability and high-quality training for every student.

## More Than Just Food
EDC Rice Farm Harvest
“Each grain harvested is more than just food — it represents the Centre's commitment to agricultural innovation.”
The EDC rice farm reached harvest, a milestone for the Centre's agricultural entrepreneurship initiative. Under a hot sun, the team separated the rice grains from their stalks by hand, work that reflects perseverance, teamwork and the spirit of enterprise, after months of planting and nurturing.
“Each grain harvested is more than just food,” the Centre's message read. It represents its commitment to agricultural innovation, youth empowerment and sustainable entrepreneurship. The exercise showed the Centre's approach in practice: hands-on entrepreneurship training, value addition and community engagement, combining local expertise with modern processing to build sustainable agribusiness.
